FAMOTIDINE (Card #56)

- Likely correct: "20–40 mg IV" (mg/kg is the peds dosing pattern; standard adult IV famotidine is 20mg)
- Action: Entered as "mg" in drugs.js — verify against protocol

AMIODARONE (Cordarone)
- Added WPW with A-Fib as CI — card may list WPW broadly; the specific danger is WPW + A-Fib (unblocked accessory pathway → can precipitate VF). Amiodarone can be used in WPW with regular VT.
- Added iodine allergy note to hypersensitivity CI — amiodarone contains iodine.
- Added digoxin interaction to precautions — amiodarone can double digoxin plasma levels.
- No CIs in pulseless cardiac arrest — noted explicitly per Chicago EMS and ACLS guidelines.

ADENOSINE (Adenocard)
- WPW CI clarified — contraindicated specifically in WPW with A-Fib (rapid accessory pathway conduction → VF risk). Can be used in WPW with regular narrow SVT.
- Added bronchospasm/asthma as CI — FDA label explicitly contraindicates in active bronchoconstriction; EMS-relevant.
- Added heart transplant / dipyridamole / carbamazepine note — use 3 mg starting dose; exaggerated or prolonged response expected.
- Caffeine/theophylline interaction added to precautions — competitively block adenosine receptors.

ACTIVATED CHARCOAL (Actidose)
- Added timing emphasis (within 1 hour) — card mentions this but it's the single most important clinical consideration. Added to precautions.
- Added caustic and hydrocarbon CIs explicitly — charcoal does not bind these and increases aspiration/injury risk.
- Unprotected airway listed as first CI — aspiration risk is the primary prehospital concern.

ACETAMINOPHEN (Tylenol / Ofirmev)
- Added 2 g/day max for high-risk patients (elderly, chronic alcohol use, hepatic impairment) — standard 4 g/day max can cause toxicity in these groups. EMS-relevant when patients have multiple Tylenol-containing products in history.

BUMETANIDE (Bumex)
- Added Anuria as CI — explicitly contraindicated per FDA label; not on card.
- Added sulfonamide allergy warning to precautions — FDA warning, cross-sensitivity possible (same as furosemide).
- Added Ototoxicity to adverse effects — class effect, less than furosemide at equivalent diuretic doses but risk exists IV especially at high doses or with aminoglycosides.
- Added Hypokalemia to adverse effects — primary electrolyte danger; card had "ECG Changes" which is a downstream consequence.

ATROPINE SULFATE
- "2nd or 3rd Degree Blocks" corrected — card lists both as absolute CIs but 2nd degree Type I (Wenckebach/Mobitz I) is actually an indication for atropine. Only Type II (Mobitz II) and 3rd degree with wide QRS are contraindicated (infranodal blocks where atropine can paradoxically worsen or cause V-fib). Updated CIs accordingly.
- Narrow-Angle Glaucoma downgraded to relative CI — multiple EMS protocols list no absolute CIs for indicated emergency use.

ASPIRIN (ASA)
- Duration "3–6 hrs" misleading — this reflects analgesic duration, not antiplatelet effect. Antiplatelet mechanism is irreversible, lasting the life of the platelet (~7–10 days). Updated to clarify.
- "May repeat after 4 hrs" not standard EMS practice for ACS — aspirin is a single dose for cardiac events. Left in data per card but noted here.

CALCIUM CHLORIDE
- V-Fib During Resuscitation changed to relative CI — card lists as absolute, but calcium chloride IS indicated if V-fib is caused by hyperkalemia. Standard phrasing per OKC/LA County: contraindicated unless known hyperkalemia.

DEXAMETHASONE (Decadron)
- Onset corrected: card says 15–30 min, but anti-inflammatory effect onset is 4–6 hours per OKC EMS protocol and StatPearls. Updated to "4–6 hrs (anti-inflammatory effect)".
- Peds dose corrected: card says 0.1–0.5 mg/kg; standard EMS protocols (OKC, Maine EMS) use 0.6 mg/kg max 10 mg. Updated accordingly.
- GI Bleeding and Active Infection downgraded to relative CIs — not standard absolute CIs for single-dose emergency prehospital use.

DEXTROSE (D10W / D25W / D50W)
- Typo on card: "Intercranial" corrected to "Intracranial".
- Intracranial Hemorrhage marked as relative CI — some protocols allow dextrose if hypoglycemia is confirmed even with ICH.
- Added AMS and seizures of unknown etiology to indications — standard EMS indications not on card.

DOBUTAMINE (Dobutrex)
- Marked Acute MI as relative CI — card lists absolute, but used cautiously post-MI in practice per references.
- Added sulfite allergy to hypersensitivity CI and precautions — dobutamine contains sulfite preservative, EMS-relevant.
- Added β-2 vasodilation to MOA — card says "minimal alpha activity" but the afterload reduction is actually β-2 mediated.
- Note: "Severe Hypotension" as indication is debatable — most references prefer dopamine first when SBP <70-80. Dobutamine shines in CHF/cardiogenic shock. Left in per card.

DROPERIDOL (Inapsine)
- Added Pheochromocytoma as CI — not on card, but FDA label warns of severe hypertension and tachycardia in these patients.
- Added Akathisia, Tachycardia, NMS to adverse effects — all well-documented but missing from card.
- Added "Butyrophenone" to drug classes — card only listed Antipsychotic/Antiemetic, but class matters clinically (related to haloperidol).
- MOA: Card says "blocks dopamine receptors" — specified as D2 receptors per pharmacology references.
- FDA Black Box Warning emphasized in precautions — card mentions QT prolongation but significantly underplays the severity. Fatal torsade de pointes reported at or below recommended doses.

HYDROMORPHONE (Dilaudid)
- ⚠️ CRITICAL: Card says peds dose is "0.2–1 mg/kg" — this is almost certainly a life-threatening typo. Multiple EMS references confirm peds hydromorphone is 0.01–0.02 mg/kg (max 0.5 mg/dose). 0.2–1 mg/kg would be a 10–100x overdose in a child. Corrected in drugs.js — MUST verify against authoritative source before publishing.
- Duration: Card says 2–3 hrs. References (including FDA label, OKC EMS protocol) say 4–6 hrs for IV. Corrected in drugs.js.
- "Overdose" removed from adverse effects — not a standard AE, it's a risk/precaution.

HYDRALAZINE (Apresoline)
- "Headache" missing from adverse effects on card — added (one of the most commonly cited AEs).
- Card otherwise accurate. Contraindications confirmed by multiple sources.

HEPARIN SODIUM
- ⚠️ Card lists "Ischemic Stroke" as an indication — REMOVED. Cannot rule out hemorrhagic stroke in prehospital setting; heparin in hemorrhagic stroke is catastrophic. Most EMS protocols explicitly exclude stroke patients. Verify with local protocol before adding back.
- DVT and PE listed as prehospital indications — unusual; most systems start heparin for these in-hospital. Left in drugs.js but flagged.
- Adult dose (70–80 units/kg): some EMS protocols use 60 units/kg as the standard. Max of 4,000 units is consistent. Entered as card states.
- Missing contraindications added: active anticoagulant use (warfarin/DOACs), aortic dissection, recent surgery.

HALOPERIDOL (Haldol)
- Card missing "Dementia with Lewy Bodies" as a contraindication — added. Same severity as Parkinson's per FDA labeling.
- NMS (Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome) missing from adverse effects — added. Rare but potentially fatal.

GLUCAGON (GlucaGen)
- Card lists "Hyperglycemia" as a contraindication — not supported by standard references; entered as relative CI. Real absolute CIs (missing from card): pheochromocytoma, insulinoma — added to drugs.js.
- Peds CCB/BB maintenance drip: card says "0.05–0.10 mg/hr" — almost certainly a typo for "0.05–0.10 mg/kg/hr". Entered as mg/kg/hr — verify against protocol.

FUROSEMIDE (Lasix)
- Adult dose: Card uses weight-based (0.5–1 mg/kg). Many EMS protocols use fixed doses (40 mg / 80 mg depending on whether patient is already on a diuretic). Entered as card states — verify against local protocol.
- Duration: Card says 4–6 hrs. Multiple EMS references say ~2 hrs for IV. 4–6 hrs is oral dosing. Consider correcting to 2 hrs.
- Ototoxicity missing from adverse effects on card — added to drugs.js since it's clinically significant (esp. with rapid infusion).
- Sulfonamide hypersensitivity not listed on card — added to contraindications since furosemide is a sulfonamide derivative.

FLUMAZENIL (Card #?)

Real absolute CIs: hypersensitivity, benzo used for life-threatening condition (ICP/status epilepticus), TCA overdose signs. Entered as written on card — verify against protocol.
- "Seizures" was MISSING from adverse effects on the card — added to drugs.js since it's the primary safety concern (Box Warning per FDA). Confirm this should be included.
